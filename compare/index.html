Options compared

Keeping your Snapchat Memories: your three real options

Now that free Memories storage is capped, there are three honest ways to hold onto your photos and videos: pay Snapchat to keep them, do the export and sorting by hand, or let an app do it once. Here’s the trade-off, without the sales pitch.

They aren’t all the same kind of choice. Paying Snapchat keeps your memories exactly where they are and asks for a fee on repeat. The other two move a copy into your own photo library, where there’s nothing left to pay: one does it with your own effort, the other automates it. None of them is wrong; the right one depends on how big your library is and whether you want the bill to end.

Is it cheaper to pay Snapchat or use an app to keep my Memories?

Over time, a one-time purchase is cheaper than a recurring fee. Snapchat’s storage plan is billed on repeat and keeps your memories on Snapchat’s servers only for as long as you keep paying. A one-time app is paid once and puts the memories in your own photo library, where there is nothing left to pay and nothing left to lose if you cancel anything. If you never mind paying monthly and want everything to stay inside Snapchat, the plan is the simplest path; if you want the bill to end, moving a copy to your phone does that.

Can I keep my Snapchat Memories for free?

Yes, the official export is free, and so is restoring the dates by hand. Request your data from Snapchat, download the zip files, unzip them on a computer, and use a free tool like ExifTool to write each memory’s real date back from the memories_history.json file. The only cost is your time, and for a small library that trade is fine. It gets tedious fast for a large one, which is the gap the app fills.

The full free method (switches to turn on, the file to read, the tool to use) is written out in the export guide.

What’s the difference between Snapchat’s storage plan and saving to my phone?

A storage plan rents space on Snapchat’s servers; saving to your phone gives you an unconditional copy. With the plan, your memories stay in Snapchat and stay only while you keep paying. Saved to your own photo library, they sit alongside your camera photos with their real dates and places, work offline, and stay yours whatever Snapchat charges or changes later. The two aren’t mutually exclusive: you can keep using Snapchat and still hold your own copy.

If the storage limit is what brought you here, the 5GB limit page explains exactly what’s changed and what happens to over-limit memories.

Why not just export and sort my Memories myself?

You can, and for a small library it’s a fine afternoon. The manual route means unzipping every export part on a computer, matching each file to its entry in memories_history.json, writing the date back with a metadata tool, and recombining caption overlays with their photos by hand. That’s workable for a few dozen memories and grim for a few thousand; the app automates exactly those steps on the phone, and the first 100 are free so you can compare the result before paying.
