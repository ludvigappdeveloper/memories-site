Storage limit

Snapchat’s 5GB Memories limit: what it means, and what to do

Snapchat’s free Memories storage is now capped. Here’s what the 5GB limit actually is, what happens to memories over it, and the calmest way to make sure yours are safe, without a monthly fee.

If you got a notification that your Snapchat Memories are running out of space, you don’t need to panic, but you shouldn’t ignore it either. The short version: the years of photos and videos you’ve saved in Memories used to be free and unlimited, and now they aren’t. This page explains exactly what changed, and walks you to the one thing worth doing about it: keeping your own copy, in your own photo library, where no limit can reach it.

What is Snapchat’s 5GB Memories limit?

In late 2025 Snapchat ended unlimited Memories. Free accounts now get a fixed amount of storage (5 GB at the announcement), and anything beyond that requires a paid storage plan. The limit covers the photos and videos saved in Memories; the exact terms and figure are set by Snapchat and can change again.

For a light user, 5 GB may be years of headroom. For anyone who has treated Memories as their real photo album (trips, friends, everyday snaps), it fills up faster than it sounds, because videos are heavy. Once you’re over the line, Snapchat starts asking you to pay.

Will Snapchat actually delete my Memories?

Not on the spot. Snapchat has said memories over your limit are moved to temporary storage for a grace period rather than deleted immediately, but they are on a deletion path unless you free up space or start paying. The safe reading is that over-limit memories are no longer guaranteed to stay forever.

That’s the real shift. The comfort of “it’ll just be there” is gone, replaced by a condition: your memories stay as long as you’re under the limit or paying to be over it. Conditions like that are exactly what you don’t want standing between you and your own past.

Do I have to pay Snapchat to keep my Memories?

Only if you want to keep them inside Snapchat. A Snapchat storage plan is a recurring charge that keeps your memories on Snapchat’s servers for as long as you keep paying. The alternative is to move a copy into your own phone once, and stop renting the space. A copy in your own photo library has no storage limit and no monthly fee.

Both can be true at the same time: you can keep using Snapchat however you like and hold your own copy, so that whatever Snapchat charges or changes later, your memories are already safe on your device. If you’re weighing the two, we lay them out side by side on the options compared page.

How do I get my Memories out before anything is deleted?

Request your data through Snapchat’s official My Data export, download the zip files it emails you, and save the photos and videos into your iPhone’s photo library. That copy is unconditionally yours: no limit, no monthly fee, no account. The full step-by-step is in the export guide.

There is one trap worth naming here: when you request the export, turn on Export JSON Files as well as Export your Memories. That JSON file is what carries each memory’s real date and location. Without it, everything imports stamped with today’s date instead of the day it happened. The guide shows the exact switches.

How long do I have to save my Snapchat Memories?

There is no public countdown, so treat it as: sooner is safer. Two clocks matter: the grace period on over-limit memories, and the roughly 7-day expiry on each export download link Snapchat generates. The export itself only takes an evening, so there is no reason to leave it on the deletion path.

The second clock catches people out: once Snapchat builds your export and emails the download links, those links go dead after about a week. If you request it, get busy, and come back a fortnight later, you’ll have to start the request over. Do it in one sitting, on Wi-Fi.

What’s the safest way to keep my Snapchat Memories for free?

The export is free; the only cost is your time. Once the zip files are on your phone you can restore the real dates and captions by hand with free desktop tools, or let an app do it in one pass. Either way the memories end up in your own photo library, where no storage limit applies. Saving snaps one by one inside Snapchat is not practical for a large library, and the manual save caps at roughly 100 items at a time.

The by-hand route is real and the export guide covers it: unzip the parts on a computer, read memories_history.json, and write each date back with a free tool like ExifTool. It’s workable for a small library and grim for a big one. Memories: Save to Photos does the same job on the phone (dates, places and captions restored in one pass), and the first 100 memories are free, so you can confirm it works on your own export before paying anything.

One switch so new snaps never get stuck again

The export handles your history. For everything from today on, flip one setting in Snapchat: Settings → Memories → Save Destinations → Memories & Camera Roll. Every snap you save from then on also lands in your photo library at the same time, so it’s never locked behind a storage limit in the first place. Your back catalogue still needs the export, but only once.

“My Data”, “Export your Memories”, “Export JSON Files”, “Save Destinations” and “Memories & Camera Roll” are labels in Snapchat’s own interface, referenced here so you can find them.
